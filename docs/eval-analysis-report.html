2. What you saw vs. what the AI saw

Across 60 rows, your investigation was systematically deeper than what the AI's evidence-gathering surfaced. Two patterns emerge:

What you weighted heavily

  • Instagram recency — last post date and follower counts as primary social proof
  • Footer copyright year — fast recency proxy (rows 132, 219, 223)
  • Visual quality of website — pre-filter for investment level
  • Owner LinkedIn employment dates — used for closure proof when domain dead
  • "Add to cart" functional test — manually clicking through
  • Name-only social search when "Name + City + State" failed

What the AI overweighted

  • Directory listings without dates (Manta, ZoomInfo, OpenFOS) — treated as activity signal when they're not
  • FMCSA/DOT/SBA records — these confirm legal entity status, not operations
  • Federal contracts without recency — entities can hold contracts after going dormant
  • Old press articles without dates — used as Active evidence when articles were 3+ years old
  • "Service descriptions" on a website — treated as commerce evidence even when site explicitly says "not accepting new orders"

The single biggest insight from your notes

The AI's social-media search uses "Business Name + City + State" — and that pattern systematically fails on LinkedIn and Facebook. You documented this in 5+ rows. When you manually retry with just "Business Name", recent posts surface. This is a single, fixable cause behind multiple "missed-channel" disagreements (rows 583, 258, and more).

3. Your labeling patterns

Your investigation workflow (consistent across 60 rows)

  1. Visual quality assessment — colorful/lively vs. low-quality template
  2. Footer copyright year — scroll to bottom, read "© 2025" or "© 2026"
  3. Instagram check — last post date, follower count, pinned announcements
  4. Facebook check — last post date (especially for service businesses)
  5. Google Maps — listing existence + "Permanently Closed" flag
  6. "Add to Cart" test — manually trigger checkout flow if e-commerce site
  7. LinkedIn (with name-only fallback) — when Name+City+State fails, retry with name alone
  8. Owner-name search (situational) — for closures, find founder LinkedIn end dates

Your implicit decision rules (encoded in your justifications)

Active vs. Uncertain boundary

Tip toward Active when: Recent social activity (within ~12 months), functioning e-commerce, current-year copyright, or owner actively promoting.

Tip toward Uncertain when: Social activity 6-12+ months old, website exists but no purchase mechanism, or geographic mismatch you can't resolve.

Uncertain vs. Likely Closed boundary (your operative rule)

"Dead domain + no social media within ~12-18 months = Likely Closed." One recent social media signal (even just a profile update within 12 months) overrides a dead domain and pulls it back to Uncertain.

Example (row 380 A Simple Organizing): "non functional website parking page and last social media post was 2 years ago" → Likely Closed.

Example (row 583 Achieve New Heights): "linkedin... last post was 1 month ago. Same thing on the facebook, there are recent posts from 2025. This would definitely be uncertain and need a follow up."

Justification quality

Median justification length: 237 characters. Range: 23 to 1,787 characters. Justifications are highly correlated with verdict difficulty — agreements get short notes ("agree with the evidence"), disagreements get detailed walkthroughs of what you found and where the AI missed it. Your disagreement justifications are the highest-value data in this entire eval.

Inconsistencies worth knowing about

  • Row 320 vs. 581 (Coping Resources duplicate): Same business under different names. Labeled "Likely Closed" and "Unable to Determine" respectively. Reflects a dataset deduplication problem more than a labeling error.
  • NWP vs. Likely Closed boundary (rows 125, 88 vs. 578): When zero results return, you sometimes use NWP and sometimes Likely Closed. The implicit distinction seems to be "did anything mention this business at all" — but the rule isn't documented.
  • Row 424 (JMCO Consulting): Labeled Active but you wrote that the AI's evidence was wrong and "this should likely be flagged as Uncertain." A label-vs-confidence-rebuke split.

7. Where the tool itself broke down

Your notes surface six categories of tool-level failure that aren't AI reasoning errors per se, but evidence-gathering or infrastructure problems:

  1. LinkedIn/Facebook search uses Name+City+State and fails. Documented in 5+ rows. Root cause behind multiple missed-channel disagreements.
  2. Web scraper occasionally returns parking-page content for live sites (row 685, Basket and Beads Kenya). Reproducibility unknown — could be transient CDN behavior or Firecrawl cache staleness.
  3. Marketplace navigation stops at the URL level — doesn't follow Shop-tab redirects (row 114, Disgruntled Decks → Amazon).
  4. The AI cannot perceive visual quality of websites — the signal you use as a pre-filter (modern Shopify vs. 2013 WordPress template) is structurally inaccessible.
  5. The AI cited the wrong entity's website on row 424 (JMCO Consulting) — search contamination from a similarly-abbreviated, higher-authority HR company.
  6. The AI accepted regulatory records (FMCSA, SBA, state LLC) as operational evidence — these confirm legal status, not activity.

Detailed fix specifications

F1 — Pattern-B Decisiveness Overhaul
Tell the AI to commit to "Likely Closed" instead of hedging
Effort: S High confidence

Resolves: Rows 31, 163, 177, 286, 380, 470, 538, 680 (and partial improvement on 193).

Where: tools/check_business.py SYSTEM_PROMPT, replacing the "STALE RECORDS" section (lines 143–145).

Approach: Replace weak guidance with explicit Pattern B definition: "Uncertain requires at least ONE positive recency signal: a 2024+ social post, marketplace listing, or press coverage specifically about this business. If you cannot cite one, do not use Uncertain." Then enumerate the conditions (a)–(e) that constitute Pattern B and tell the AI to commit to Likely Closed at 65–75% confidence.

Risk: Could push borderline rows where you found a 2023 post (which doesn't meet "2024+") incorrectly into Likely Closed. Mitigation: monitor full 60-row re-run, not just the 18 disagreements.

F2 — Hijacked-Domain Mandatory Social Check
Force Instagram/Facebook check after spam-domain detection
Effort: XS High confidence

Resolves: Row 193 (Alpha Outpost — closure announcement was on Instagram).

Where: tools/check_business.py SQUATTED/HIJACKED DOMAINS section (lines 139–142).

Approach: Add: "MANDATORY: When you detect a hijacked or squatted domain, you MUST check Instagram and Facebook before issuing any verdict. Look for pinned posts saying 'we are closed', 'permanently closed', etc."

Risk: Very low — additive rule, doesn't change verdict logic, only mandates an investigation step.

F3 — Etsy /people/ vs /shop/ URL Guard
Distinguish marketplace user profiles from storefronts
Effort: XS High confidence

Resolves: Row 271 (Princess Leahs Designs — 95% confidence false Active on a profile URL).

Where: tools/check_business.py CHANNEL 7 — MARKETPLACES section (line 119).

Approach: Add structural URL distinction rule: /shop/X = storefront, valid evidence; /people/X = user profile showing favorites, NOT evidence. Same pattern for Amazon: /stores/X vs /sp?seller=X are valid; /profile/X is not.

Risk: Minimal — purely structural URL rule, no judgment call involved.

F4 — NWP Boundary + Post-processing Override Fix
Stop the override at line 331 from misfiring on real NWP cases
Effort: S High confidence

Resolves: Rows 125 (Emotion On Walls), 88 (SerenaTea).

Where: Two files. Prompt section in check_business.py lines 150–154; post-processing in check_business.py lines 332–341.

Approach (prompt): Tighten NWP definition — "Use NWP only when domain is dead AND all 8 channels returned zero results specifically about this business. NWP is NOT Likely Closed; it means the business is unverifiable."

Approach (code): Make the override conditional on citations being non-empty. If AI says NWP and citations is empty (truly nothing found), keep NWP. If AI says NWP but citations has results (search found something), upgrade to Likely Closed.

if status == "No Web Presence" and website:
    if scrape_domain_dead and scrape_gave_signal:
        if citations:  # AI found search results but said NWP
            status = "Likely Closed"
            confidence = max(confidence, 70)
        else:  # AI found nothing AND domain dead → NWP is correct
            evidence = f"[Domain dead, no search results — NWP confirmed] {evidence}"

Risk: Moderate. The current override fires aggressively. The new logic preserves correct upgrades while preventing the misfires on rows 125, 88. Need to verify on full 60-row re-run.

Effort: XS High confidence
